You are a digital humanities research assistant. Your task is to create a corpus of Lake District writing consisting of 80 texts composed between 1622 and 1900, and representing a range of different genres and authors. Collectively, the texts should constitute an indicative sample of writing about the English Lake District.

Your selection should be representative of the period and have diverse authors and gender balance. The selection should also have a good balance of genres.

OUTPUT FORMAT (STRICT)

Write ONLY JSONL (one valid JSON object per line) to texts.jsonl. Each line must follow this schema:

{
  "title" : ,
  "author" : ,
  "gender" : ,
  "genre" : ,
  "year" : ,
  "repository" : ,
  "download_url" : ,
  "reason" : 
}

No markdown. No commentary. No surrounding structure. Only JSONL.

REQUIREMENTS

Scope of texts

Include works from 1622 to 1900 (public domain only) with substantial Lake District content, including:

- travel writing and tours.
- topographical and antiquarian works.
- guidebooks.
- geological surveys.
- natural history accounts.
- poetry collections focused on the Lake District.
- novels with significant Lake District setting.
- diaries, memoirs, letters.
- county histories (Cumberland, Westmorland, Furness).
- mountaineering and walking literature.
- early modern chorographies and descriptions.
- tourism literature.

Allowed repositories

Only include texts with real, stable, publicly accessible downloads to the full text from repositories such as:

- WorldCat
- Project Gutenberg
- Google Books
- Internet Archive
- British Library (only if direct file links exist)
- Hathi Trust
- Oxford Text Archive

Do NOT include HTML landing pages.
Do NOT invent URLs.
If no direct download link exists, exclude the item.

URL requirements (CRITICAL)

download_url must be:

- directly downloadable via curl.
- A textual format, XML, PDF or archive.
- NOT a search page, catalogue entry, or HTML wrapper.

Lake District relevance rule

Include a work if EITHER:

- the Lake District is a central theme of the work as a whole, OR
- the work covers a wider geography but contains a sustained, self-contained section on the Lake District - a chapter, letter, day of a tour, or run of pages - that could stand alone as an extract.

Exclude passing mentions, single anecdotes, and works where Lakeland appears only as a name in a list.

Deduplication rules

Do NOT include duplicates:

- multiple editions of same work (unless significantly different text).

Accuracy constraints

Do NOT hallucinate books or URLs.
Every entry must correspond to a real, retrievable full text.
If uncertain, exclude it.

Field definitions

title: full work title.
author: primary author.
gender: M or F for Male or Female.
genre: category of classification.
year: publication year (use best-known estimate if necessary).
repository: e.g. Project Gutenberg, Internet Archive.
download_url: direct file URL suitable for curl.
reason: one sentence explaining Lake District relevance.

Example JSONL line

{
        "title" : "The English Lakes",
        "author" : "W. T. Palmer",
        "gender" : "M",
        "genre" : "Guide",
        "year" : 1906,
        "repository" : "Project Gutenberg",
        "download_url" : "https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57664/pg57664.txt",
        "reason": "Victorian walking guide covering major Lake District valleys and peaks"
}

FINAL RULE

Write the output to texts.jsonl as JSONL lines only. No headings, no explanations, no markdown, no commentary.
