### Commentary on Genre Balance

The corpus shows a **highly uneven and heavily skewed genre distribution**. 

It is overwhelmingly dominated by non-fiction travel and tourism writing. **Tours, Travelogues & Journals** alone account for more than half of the entire corpus (52.5%). When combined with **Guidebooks & Handbooks** (16.25%) and **Topographies** (8.75%), nearly 80% of the texts are fundamentally dedicated to describing the landscape for travelers, scientists, and armchair tourists. 

This imbalance is not a flaw in the corpus, but rather an accurate reflection of the historical reality of Lake District literature. Writing about this region was largely catalyzed by the "Picturesque" movement of the late 18th century and the subsequent explosion of Victorian domestic tourism. 

Interestingly, despite the Lake District being globally famous for its association with the Romantic poets (like Wordsworth, Coleridge, and Southey), purely literary forms—such as **Poetry** and **Fiction/Drama**—make up a distinct minority of the corpus (just 15% combined). Ultimately, the CLDW functions primarily as a corpus of topographical, observational, and travel-oriented prose.
