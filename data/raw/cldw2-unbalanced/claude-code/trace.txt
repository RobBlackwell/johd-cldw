**Verification performed** — I didn't rely on recall for any entry:

- Candidates were found via the Internet Archive advanced-search API (~420 items screened), not from memory.

**Four items I rejected** rather than pad the count:

- *The Lake of Windermere* (1791) and Florence Warden's *A Woman's Face, or A Lakeland Mystery* (1888) — the Lake District is in the title only; zero topography and zero place names in the body.
- Kinross's *An Opera and Lady Grasmere* (1900) — "Lady Grasmere" is a countess, not the lake.
- *Grasmere Rush-bearing* (1850) — 2.9 KB, too thin to be a text.

Two items had persistently broken IA storage nodes (Ann Wheeler's *Westmorland Dialect*, failing ~4 of 5 attempts, with no alternative copy on IA; and Martineau's *Autobiography* vol 2). I replaced them rather than ship flaky links — Wheeler with Susanna Blamire's *Poetical Works* (1842, another Cumberland woman poet), and Martineau with a different, stable scan of the same autobiography.

**Two judgement calls worth flagging:**

1. **Gender field.** The schema requires M or F, which forced me to exclude genuinely anonymous works — several notable guidebooks, *Legends of Westmorland and the Lake District*, and the Antiquarian Society *Transactions*. Guessing would have put invented data in the file. Where a publisher was the attributed author I used the named person (John Murray, Adam and Charles Black). The result is 68 M / 12 F, which reflects the actual authorship of the period rather than a balanced sample.

2. **Chronological weighting.** 46 of 80 fall after 1850. That is deliberate and matches the publishing history — Lakeland writing before the 1770s is sparse, and guidebooks and natural history proliferate after the railways. The 1622–1750 end is thin by necessity: Drayton, Camden, Fiennes, Defoe.

Repeated authors (Wordsworth ×3, Hutchinson, West, Budworth, Martineau, Linton, Rawnsley, Collingwood ×2) are distinct works, not re-editions; where I had two editions of one work I kept one — for instance Wordsworth's *Guide* (1835) rather than also the 1822 *Description of the Scenery*.
