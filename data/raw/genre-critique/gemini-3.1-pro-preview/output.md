### Commentary on Genre Balance

The corpus exhibits a **highly uneven and heavily skewed genre distribution**. 

Rather than a balanced cross-section of all literary forms, the CLDW is overwhelmingly dominated by non-fiction, travel-oriented, and topographical prose. 
* **Dominance of Touristic Writing:** "Travelogues" and "Guides" alone account for more than half of the corpus (56.25%). When combined with other life-writing and observational genres like Journals, Epistles, and Essays, topographical and travel literature accounts for approximately 80% of the entire collection.
* **Underrepresentation of Fictional/Imaginative Genres:** Traditional imaginative genres make up a much smaller footprint. While "Poetry" maintains a modest presence (10%), narrative fiction (spread thinly across "Fiction," "Novel," and "Prose Fiction") accounts for less than 4% of the corpus collectively, and "Drama" represents barely 1%. 

This uneven distribution is entirely logical given the thematic focus of the corpus. Because it is specifically engineered to capture writing *about* a geographical region (the English Lake District) between the 17th and 20th centuries, it naturally reflects the historical reality that this landscape primarily attracted tourists, antiquarians, topographers, and guidebook compilers.
